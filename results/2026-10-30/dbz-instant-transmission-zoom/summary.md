# DBZ-style Instant Transmission zoom transition

The recognizable look is a **snap zoom + directional blur + white flash + a few-frame absence/afterimage**, rather than one stock transition. You can build it in Premiere, After Effects, DaVinci Resolve, or CapCut; After Effects gives the most control.

## Edit recipe

1. **Shoot two locked shots** on a tripod: one with the subject present and one clean plate with them absent. Keep exposure and framing fixed.
2. **Cut on the action:** place the disappearance 2–4 frames before the flash; use the clean plate immediately after.
3. **Create the zoom:** keyframe Scale from 100% to roughly 170–250% over 4–8 frames, aimed at the subject’s departure point. Add strong motion/directional blur and ease the curve aggressively.
4. **Flash and smear:** place a 1–3-frame white solid/additive flash over the cut; duplicate the outgoing frame, blur/scale it briefly, and lower opacity for a streak.
5. **Sound sells it:** use a rising whoosh ending in a sharp transient, then leave a short air-gap before the destination impact.
6. **Arrival:** reverse the sequence—flash, blurred incoming frame, then subject appears—with a small camera shake only on the landing frame.

## Tool-specific shortcut

- **CapCut/Premiere:** keyframe scale and position, use the built-in motion blur/zoom or directional blur effect, then add a white color matte. “Pull In”/zoom presets can be a starting point, but hand-tuned keyframes will match the footage better.
- **After Effects:** pre-compose the departing shot; animate Scale/Position, add Directional Blur or CC Force Motion Blur, and use an adjustment layer for flash/shake. Use rotoscoping only when the camera moves; otherwise the clean plate cut is cleaner.

Avoid making it a long smooth zoom: Instant Transmission should feel discontinuous and faster than the viewer can track. The effect’s intent is supported by a cut, flash, motion blur, and sound design—not by extreme zoom alone.
